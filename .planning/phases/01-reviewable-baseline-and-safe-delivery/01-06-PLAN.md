<design_decision>
## What rule applies inside `en/` — the load-bearing decision

`en/` is OPTIONAL per D-07 and its contents are not enumerable today; Phase 10 authors them. Two
rules therefore apply at different depths, and conflating them would either leave the hole open or
break Phase 10 before it starts:

- **Top level of the staged root — positive allowlist (default-deny).** Every entry directly under
  `_site/` must appear in `REQUIRED` or `OPTIONAL`. This is the assertion that makes an unlisted
  file fail instead of ship, and it is the mirror of D-05: the staging script decides what is
  copied, the checker asserts that nothing else arrived.
- **Every depth, including inside `en/` — forbidden basename plus no symlinks.** A full recursive
  allowlist would require enumerating a directory tree that does not exist yet, and would turn
  Phase 10's first commit red. Rejecting forbidden basenames at any depth and rejecting every
  symlink is what `01-VERIFICATION.md` asks for and is the strongest rule that can be stated
  without knowing `en/`'s contents.

The two rules overlap on some entries — a top-level dangling `README.md` symlink trips all three
checks and produces three annotations. That redundancy is deliberate. Do not suppress one rule with
another to make the output tidier; each must be independently able to fail the run.

Symlinks matter beyond tidiness: `actions/upload-pages-artifact` tars with `--dereference`, so a
symlink inside a future `en/` pointing at `../../.planning` would have its target's contents
published while a name-only comparison saw nothing wrong.
</design_decision>
